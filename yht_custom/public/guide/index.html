How to read this

Two parts. Part A is for anyone raising documents at a branch — read it start to finish once, then use it to look things up. Part B is for whoever administers the system; it assumes Part A.

Every screenshot is from the live system, and every rule was checked against it rather than written from memory. Where something is deliberately not switched on yet, it says so instead of pretending otherwise.

If the screen disagrees with this page, trust the screen. Then tell us, because it means this page needs a new version.

A3 · Getting back to it

Two ways, both available from any screen:

  • Click ← Dashboard in the top bar, to the right of the search box.
  • Click the Yaser Hameed logo in the top-left corner.
You do not need the browser Back button. Either route above returns you cleanly. Browser Back can drop you onto a half-loaded screen or into a form you had started editing.

If you land somewhere your role does not cover, you are not shown an error page — an orange message names the screen and you are returned to the dashboard. That is not a fault and you have not broken anything.

A4 · Deliver first, then bill

The single most important rule on this system. Stock leaves the branch on a Delivery Note. A Sales Invoice only bills what has already left. One movement, one record — never both on one document.

Standard ERPNext lets you tick Update Stock on an invoice and move stock from there. That is switched off here on purpose, and the invoice tells you so:

The reason is that it keeps the stock ledger and the sales ledger honest. When stock and money move on one document, correcting one quietly corrupts the other.

Step 1 — the Delivery Note

  1. From the submitted Sales Order: Create → Delivery Note. Items, quantities and rates carry over.
  2. No order? Dashboard → Delivery Note → + Add.
  3. Check the quantity actually going out. Change it here if it differs — this is the record of what physically left.
  4. The Stock column shows what is on hand in your warehouse for that item, live, as you type. If it reads less than you are about to deliver, stop — the submit will refuse it rather than go negative.
  5. Submit. Stock leaves Stores - KATC at this moment.
  6. Print — customer copy and office copy, with a signature block.
A submitted Delivery Note is locked. A delivery line also cannot carry a zero rate, and the system will not let you go into negative stock. Get the quantity right before you submit. If it is wrong afterwards, that needs a return document — ask your manager.

Step 2 — the Sales Invoice

  1. Open the submitted Delivery Note → Create → Sales Invoice.
  2. The items arrive already linked to the delivery. Do not retype them.
  3. Set Payment Mode — see A6.
  4. Check Payment Due Date, which fills in from the customer's terms.
  5. Submit.
Always start from the Delivery Note. Building the invoice from the delivery keeps the two linked, so Delivery Notes To Bill on your dashboard clears correctly. An invoice typed from scratch bills the customer fine but leaves that delivery sitting in the column forever, looking unbilled.

A5 · Deciding the price

You are never guessing. Two buttons sit under the items table, next to Add Row.

Discount

Each row carries its own Discount % column, next to the rate. Enter the discount there rather than working the rate out yourself — the rate updates on its own, and the printed document then shows the customer three numbers that add up:

Gross Amountwhat the items come to at list price
Item Discountthe total you gave away across all rows
Totalgross minus the discount — what they actually owe before VAT
A whole-document discount is a separate line. The header discount prints as Invoice Discount, underneath the total. It is not added into Item Discount — keeping them apart is what lets the column add up.

Price Assist

  • Price list rate — the standard rate from Kathoom Selling Price
  • Last rate to this customer — with the date and invoice number
  • Last rate to anyone — with which customer
  • Recent range — low, high and average across recent sales
  • Valuation and margin at your rate — red if you are selling below cost
  • Stock — what is available in your warehouse right now

Two shortcuts fill the row for you: Use price list rate and Use last rate to this customer.

Show Price History

The last 30 sales of that item — date, customer, quantity, rate, invoice. Open it when a customer says "you charged me less last time". The answer is on screen in one click.

Show Purchase History

Inside Price Assist, beside Show price history, there is Show purchase history. It answers the other half of the pricing question: what did we pay. Every purchase of that item — date, supplier, quantity, the rate we paid, and the supplier's own bill number — and each row links straight to the Purchase Invoice, so if a rate looks wrong you open the bill and see it.

Use it when a customer pushes on price. Knowing what the item cost is what tells you how much room you actually have, rather than guessing and discovering the margin afterwards.
You will get a quiet nudge. Type a rate that differs from the last rate to that customer and a short message says how far above or below it is. It does not block you — it makes sure you meant it.
Margin in red means you are selling below what the stock cost. Sometimes that is deliberate. It should never be an accident.

A6 · Cash or credit

Every Sales Invoice carries a Payment Mode, under the payment due date. It is a real decision, not a formality.

Payment ModeMeansWhat happens
Credit
the default		 The customer will pay later The amount joins their outstanding balance and shows on your dashboard until paid.
CashYou are collecting now As soon as you submit, the Collect Payment dialog opens.
The trap: leaving it on Credit for a cash sale. The invoice submits perfectly happily. The money is in the drawer and the system still thinks the customer owes it, so somebody chases a customer who has already paid. Set Payment Mode before you submit.

Pick the customer and two indicators appear at the top of the invoice: Outstanding (everything they already owe) and Credit limit, shown only if finance has set one. Going over the limit warns, it does not block.

No customer has a credit limit set yet. The warning is built and waiting, but it cannot fire until finance sets limits, so you will not see it today.

A7 · Collecting the money

The dialog opens automatically after you submit a Cash invoice, and Collect Payment reopens it any time on a submitted unpaid invoice.

  1. Amount Due is shown at the top and cannot be edited.
  2. Enter what was actually tendered against each mode. Cash modes are listed first.
  3. Split it freely — part cash, part card, part transfer. Tendered and Still due update as you type.
  4. Press Record Payment. One receipt is created per mode used.

Your branch can tender against eight modes — two cash, six bank. They are listed in B2.

Not collecting today after all? Press Pay Later. The invoice stays valid and unpaid; nothing is lost.
You cannot tender more than is due. Over-tendering is refused rather than creating a credit somebody has to unpick. Give change from the drawer, not from the system.

A8 · Returns and credit notes

A return starts where the goods are, not where the money is. The same rule as A4, run backwards: stock left the branch on a delivery note, so it comes back on one, and the credit note follows.

  1. Open the Delivery Note the goods went out on → Create → Sales Return.
  2. Set the quantities actually coming back. They show as negative — that is correct, and if you type a positive number the system flips it for you.
  3. Tick Issue Credit Note before you submit.
  4. Submit. The return delivery note takes KSDR-26-…, and the credit note is created and submitted for you, numbered KSSR-26-…, already linked to it.
Starting from the invoice will be refused. If you open a Sales Invoice and use Create → Return / Credit Note for goods that were delivered on a delivery note, the system stops you and points you here. That is deliberate: a credit note raised on its own leaves the stock where it is, and nothing then records that the goods came back — so the same goods can be credited twice.
The exception: old invoices that carried their own stock. Some invoices from before the delivery-note rule moved stock themselves. There is no delivery note to return against, so those are returned on the invoice — open it, Create → Return / Credit Note, and leave Update Stock ticked. The system works out which case you are in.
A credit note never opens the payment dialog. On a return the money moves outward, so tendering makes no sense. A cash refund is a job for your manager.

A9 · Receiving goods

Buying mirrors selling: stock arrives on a Purchase Receipt, and the Purchase Invoice bills what already arrived.

  1. Dashboard → Purchase Receipt → + Add.
  2. Supplier, then the items and quantities actually received into Stores - KATC.
  3. Submit. Stock arrives at this moment.
  4. Then Create → Purchase Invoice from the receipt.
Receive what actually arrived, not what was ordered. Negative stock is not allowed, so a receipt entered short and corrected later blocks the sale in between. Count first.

A10 · Expense bills

For costs with nothing to put in stock — rent, fuel, a repair, a phone bill.

  1. Dashboard → Purchase Invoice → + Add.
  2. Tick Is Expense Invoice.
  3. Pick the Expense Head — the account this cost belongs to.
  4. Add a row per line on the supplier's bill: description and amount. No item code.
  5. Enter the supplier's own number in Supplier Invoice No.
  6. Paid on the spot in cash? Tick Is Paid and choose the account.
  7. Submit. It takes an expense number, KSEPI-26-….
The trap: putting a stock item on an expense invoice. It is refused. If what you bought goes into stock, it is not an expense — use a Purchase Receipt. Otherwise the same purchase is counted twice, once as a cost and once as inventory value.
Booking and paying are two different things. An expense invoice always credits the supplier's account. Paying it is a separate step (or the Is Paid tick for genuine over-the-counter cash). The old system mixed these, which is why cash-paid expenses never appeared in the supplier ledger.

A11 · Your reports

Seven of these are yours — built for this branch, and each answers one question. The rest are ERPNext's own. If two reports seem to answer the same thing, use the one whose name matches the question you actually have.

The Stock Ledger, General Ledger and Party Ledger tiles open the KATC versions, built to the column list you asked for. ERPNext's own versions of the first two are still there if you want them — search for them by name, or open the KATC Reports screen, which lists every report on one page.

Collection counts both ways money arrives. A Payment Entry, and cash taken at the counter on the invoice itself. Both appear, tagged in the Source column, and the filter isolates either. If you only ever looked at Payment Entries you would miss the till entirely.
Branch Receivables and Receivables Summary are not the same report. Branch Receivables lists every invoice, one row each, aged. Receivables Summary rolls those up per customer. Chasing a specific invoice: the first. Sizing the whole debt: the second.
Do not ask Stock Balance a money question. It values stock, not debt. "Who owes us?" is Receivables Summary. Asking the wrong report is the most common reason a figure looks wrong — the figure was right, the question was wrong.

Every report is filtered to your branch. Set the date range at the top before reading anything: a report showing less than you expected is usually a date range, not missing data.

A12 · Adding a customer

A walk-in with no record yet does not need the full customer form. On the Customer list, or from the New Customer tile on your dashboard, use Quick Create. One screen makes the customer, the address and the contact together.

Fill in District and Building Number. They look optional and they are not. A tax invoice to a company customer is rejected by ZATCA without them, and the rejection happens later — at invoicing, to whoever raises it, not to you now. Thirty seconds here saves someone else an argument.

The address fields follow the Saudi national address: Street, Building Number (4 digits), District, City, Postal Code (5 digits), and optionally the Short Address — the eight-character code like RQAA2929. Enter a Short Address and it becomes the address title, so the record is findable by the code the customer themselves quotes.

The form checks the shapes as you save: a three-digit building number or a two-digit postal code is refused with the reason. Leaving a field empty is allowed; putting the wrong thing in it is not.

Suppliers work the same way, but not for you. The same Quick Create button sits on the Supplier list. Your login can read suppliers and not create them, so it will tell you so rather than fail halfway.

Part B

Administrator

Configuring and maintaining the system. Assumes Part A.

B1 · Branch Configuration

One Branch Configuration record per branch. It is the single place a branch's behaviour is defined, and saving it provisions everything downstream.

Saving it:

  • creates the User Permissions that scope each listed user to the branch
  • upgrades Website users to System users where needed
  • assigns the Branch User role directly
  • applies the Module Profile that trims the desk sidebar
The first row of Warehouses and Cost Centers is the default. Both are child tables and can hold more than one row. The first is what gets filled into a branch user's documents, so order matters.
Adding a user here changes a real person's access. Add the account, save, then have that person sign out and back in — a role granted moments ago is not in their cached permission set.

B2 · Payment modes

The Modes of Payment table is the Cash/Bank allowlist for that branch's users — it is what the Collect Payment dialog offers.

These eight were seeded automatically, and the rule matters: a mode is only offered if it is enabled and has a default account for the branch's company. Twenty-one modes are enabled on this system; only eight are usable. Offering the other thirteen would let an operator allocate money and only discover the problem at submit.

Leave the table empty to apply no restriction. An unconfigured branch opts out of filtering rather than losing every mode. The moment one row is listed, the allowlist is authoritative — so it is all or nothing, on purpose.
Rows you delete by hand stay deleted. The seeding only ever runs on a branch with an empty table. Which tills a branch may touch is a human decision, and a migration has no business overruling it.

B4 · Item code prefixes

The mechanism generates an item code from its group's prefix. It is live and per-group.

No group has a prefix set yet — nought of twenty-nine. So item codes are typed by hand today. Fill in a prefix on a group and that group starts generating codes; nothing else is blocked meanwhile. This is open item B14.
A code the user typed on purpose is never overwritten. Generation only fills a blank.

Why the Sales Invoice and Delivery Note have no Arabic button. They do not need one — both already print the Arabic item name underneath the English one, inside the same cell. A separate Arabic column there would print it twice. The Arabic button exists only on the Quotation and the Sales Order, where the plain layout carries no Arabic at all.
The older YHT formats are untouched and still available from the printer icon — including YHT Delivery Note with its customer and office copies. Nothing was deleted and no doctype default was changed.
Page numbers and a page-2 letterhead do not print on this server. The PDF engine installed here is built without the patches those need, so on a document that runs past one page there is no page number, no letterhead on page two, and the column headings do not repeat. Verified by rendering a 74-line quotation. It is the engine, not the layout — changing it is a server task with its own maintenance window.
Deleting print formats is how a doctype ends up with none. ERPNext ships no format at all for Delivery Note, Quotation or Journal Entry. Journal Entry still has none here. Check what ERPNext actually provides before stripping formats.

B6 · Permissions and scoping

Three layers, and they are deliberately independent:

LayerDoesWhere
Custom DocPermDecides what the role may open at allsetup.BRANCH_USER_PERMISSIONS
Permission query conditionsDecides which rows they see in a listbranch_filters
Server-side scope guardRejects a write pointing outside the branchbranch_guard.validate_branch_scope
Client-side filters enforce nothing. The pickers narrowed on the form are convenience only. The guard on validate is the boundary, and it holds on the desk, the REST API, imports and Server Scripts alike.
A field's declared Link target can lie about what gets read. Payment Entry.party_type is declared Link → DocType, but its picker is narrowed by a query that searches Party Type — and that is the doctype permission-checked. A branch user hit a bare "No permission for Party Type" because an audit of declared Link targets could never have found it. When adding a doctype to the role, run the smoke test rather than reasoning about field definitions.

The smoke test is the check that matters — it opens every dashboard tile as a list and as a form, runs every dashboard report, exercises every picker, and asserts the claims this guide makes to you:

bench --site yht-khobhar.enfonoerp.com run-tests --app yht_custom --skip-before-tests

Always pass --skip-before-tests. Without it, every installed app's before_tests hook fires, and ERPNext's deletes every Item Price row on the site. That has already happened once here — 4,847 rows, restored from backup.

B6a · Branch Manager, and cancelled documents

There are now two branch roles. Branch User is unchanged. Branch Manager reaches everything a Branch User does — the same doctypes, the same branch scoping — plus one thing that makes it a manager.

RoleSees cancelled documents?
Branch UserNo. Cancelled documents are filtered out of every list.
Branch ManagerYes. Cancelled documents stay visible.
System ManagerYes — an administrator who cannot see a cancelled document cannot investigate one.
This is a role, not a tick box on the user. The rule is applied in the permission query itself, so it holds on list views, on search and on the API — not only on screens somebody remembered to hide.
The other five roles on the client's sheet were not built. Sales Junior, Sales Senior, Accounts Junior, Accounts Senior and a separate Administrator role were listed as "details will update later" and the client has since confirmed only Branch Manager is needed. Ask before assuming the others exist.

B7 · Deploying a change

All five steps matter, in this order:

cd /home/v15/yht-bench/apps/yht_custom && sudo -u v15 -H git pull upstream main
cd /home/v15/yht-bench && sudo -u v15 bench --site yht-khobhar.enfonoerp.com migrate
sudo -u v15 bench --site yht-khobhar.enfonoerp.com clear-cache
sudo -u v15 touch sites/assets/assets.json
sudo supervisorctl signal QUIT yht-bench-web:yht-bench-frappe-web
Three traps here all print the same misleading authentication error. The remote is upstream, not origin; sudo -u v15 needs -H; and the app directory must be chown -R v15:v15 if anything has run as root in it.
touch sites/assets/assets.json is not optional. The browser caches the whole dashboard page document in localStorage, and that cache only clears when the build version changes — which is that file's timestamp. Without it the change is invisible to anyone already signed in, forever. Clearing cache, restarting the worker and a hard refresh all fail to fix it.
The bench is /home/v15/yht-bench, not frappe-bench. frappe-bench on the same box runs other clients' live sites.
